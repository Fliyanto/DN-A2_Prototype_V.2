Unpacking the Feed

01 / 04

What happened?

The feed was never neutral.

This website is titled 404: Self Not Found, but it is masked under the friendlier identity of Pop Scroll. At first, it presents itself as a soft, colourful, and playful browsing experience.

The hidden purpose is to make users experience how a personalised feed can slowly narrow their world. What begins as choice turns into repetition, and what feels harmless slowly becomes uncomfortable.

02 / 04

How did the interaction work?

Every click became a signal.

As users click through the bubble feed, the website quietly pays attention to which categories they choose most often. The more a category is clicked, the more that type of content starts to appear.

The interaction does not need to explain itself straight away. Instead, it lets the narrowing happen gradually. Other interests begin to fade, the feed becomes darker, and the same kinds of posts return again and again.

click detected interest weight increased feed rebuilt variety reduced

03 / 04

Echo chamber

The loop starts to feel normal.

This project is based on my personal experience of being caught inside an echo chamber. Online feeds often make repeated content feel natural because they keep showing us versions of what we have already clicked, liked, watched, or paused on.

The problem is not that the feed is completely wrong. The problem is that it can become too confident. It starts treating repeated behaviour as identity, even though a person is more complicated than what they click.

04 / 04

Takeaway message

Self Not Found

The final error does not mean the user has no identity. It means the system has reduced them into a pattern it can repeat. The feed no longer sees the full person, only the version of them created through clicks.

404: Self Not Found asks users to question the comfort of personalisation. A feed can feel like it understands us, but it may only be reflecting a smaller, flatter version of who we are.

not everything you click is everything you are
